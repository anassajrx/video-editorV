# Brand guide — Software Engineer personal brand

> Fill in `name` and `handle` in `brand/brand.json` and the text in `brand/logo/wordmark.svg`.

## Positioning
- **Who:** a software engineer who builds real things and shows the proof.
- **Promise:** "Shipping code. Explaining it simply."
- **Voice:** clear, confident, builder energy. No hype; show the terminal, the numbers, the result.
- **Personality in type:** *bold sans* = the engineer, *italic serif* = the human, `mono` = the code.

## Why these colors suit you
Your footage has warm skin tones (≈ #CE9D7A / #B5896A), dark hair and beard, a white tee and a warm beige wall.
- **Signal Cyan `#38E1FF`** is the complement of warm skin, so it makes your face pop instead of fighting it. It also reads as "tech" right away (terminals, IDEs, status lights).
- **Ember `#FFB547`** repeats the warmth of your skin, so it is used for money and results: it feels like a reward.
- **Midnight Ink `#07090F`** gives depth and contrast, and keeps the cyan glow looking premium rather than neon-cheap.
- White tees work well with this palette. Avoid cyan or teal shirts, because they swallow the accent.

Ratio: ~70% footage/ink, 20% white text, 8% cyan, 2% amber. Never put cyan and amber on the same word.

## Typography (bundled in `public/fonts`, all free / OFL)
- **Inter Tight** 500/700/800: captions and titles. Tight tracking (-2 to -4).
- **Instrument Serif Italic**: emotional words ("weeks", "anyway", "a month").
- **JetBrains Mono** 400/600: labels, tags, commands, terminal cards.

## Logo
- `logo/mark.svg`: a terminal chevron `›` plus cursor `_` in a rounded square. Use it for the avatar, watermark and end card.
- `logo/wordmark.svg`: the mark, then your name, then `// software engineer`.
- Minimum clear space = the height of the cursor bar. Never recolor the chevron (always signal on ink).

## Filming setup to match the brand (cheap upgrades)
1. Put a **cyan/blue RGB light** behind you, aimed at the wall (≈$20). It turns the beige wall into a cool gradient, like the reference's blue backdrop, and separates you from the background.
2. Use a soft key light at 45° in front, slightly warm, so the skin stays natural against the cool background.
3. Frame chest-up with the eyes at about 1/3 from the top. Leave the chest area free, because cards live there.
4. Shoot vertical 4K (or 1080x1920) at 30fps, with a lav or USB mic within 20cm.
